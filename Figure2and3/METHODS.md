# Analysis methods and limits

The notebooks retain separate estimators and quality populations. Effective settings, input roles/hashes, methods and exact executed sources are saved with each run. Raw CSV files are read-only. The statements below describe code settings and documented conditions, not additional experimental verification.

## Inputs, units and preprocessing

IM `Data28` remains in counts. Selected accelerometer `Raw_Z` values use the nominal conversion 0.000488 g/count. Data11 records a reported ±16 g range and matching drive, mounting position and load; Data10 sweep configuration still has false condition/full-scale confirmation flags. Individual accelerometer frequency calibration, clock accuracy and analog bandwidth are unresolved. IM and accelerometer are independent acquisitions: comparisons use condition means, without paired-file ratios, phase, coherence or synchronized transfer functions.

The stationary FFT pipeline audits timestamps, linearly interpolates the retained N samples onto N equally spaced times spanning their original endpoints, removes the mean and applies a periodic Hann window. One-sided peak amplitude is `2*abs(FFT)/sum(window)`, without doubling DC or Nyquist. Native/padded bins and log-amplitude peak interpolation remain distinct; default padding is 8x. No additional bandpass or notch filter is applied. Invalid timestamps are not repaired; gap-interpolated estimates retain diagnostic flags. Native spacing, padded spacing and equivalent noise bandwidth (ENBW) are saved separately.

## Data10 frequency analysis and SNR

The full stepped-frequency schedule is 1-10 Hz by 1 Hz, 20-100 Hz by 10 Hz and 200-1000 Hz by 100 Hz. Blocks last 10 s from configured start 0; trimming 2 s from each end gives 6 s windows. The minimum 30 Hz selection is applied after constructing the full schedule, leaving 17 analyzed settings. IM_16 and accel_13 are independent; nothing-on controls are IM_1/accel_10 and amplifier-on controls are IM_2/accel_11. Raw plots use original counts/timestamps without FFT preprocessing.

**Nominal-band excess-power SNR (Figure 2c).** Excited power `Pe` is the integral of the native one-sided Hann periodogram over nominal frequency ±1 Hz. Each separate control gives `Pn` from a Welch PSD: 6 s periodic Hann segments, 50% overlap, segment-mean removal, no padding and arithmetic averaging of linear PSD. Both use the timestamp interpolation above; the excited window is demeaned. Piecewise-linear PSDs are integrated with matching interpolated band edges, clipped to common represented support; exact width, spacing and ENBW are recorded. Requested Welch windows are not shortened. `SNR_power=(Pe-Pn)/Pn`; dB is `10*log10(SNR_power)` only for positive noise and excess. Nonpositive excess remains recorded with unavailable SNR, without a floor. The separate measured ratio `Pe/Pn` is also retained. There is one result per excited window/control, with no pooling of controls or settings. The subtraction assumes representative additive background; in-band interference also contributes, so this is not an isolated fundamental or calibrated detection limit.

**Global peak / full-record AC RMS (supplement).** The signal is the all-positive-frequency padded global peak with three-point interpolation, divided by sqrt(2), conditional on a resolved sinusoid. Its preprocessing is the FFT pipeline above. Noise is `sqrt(mean((y-mean(y))**2))` from the original samples over the configured noise interval, normally the full record: no resampling, window, Welch averaging or filtering of this denominator. `R=(A_global/sqrt(2))/noise_rms`; dB is `20*log10(R)`, equivalently `10*log10(R**2)`, without power subtraction. Each setting/control is separate. Noise spans the recorded AC band; fs/2 is nominal digital support, not calibrated analog bandwidth. The ±1 Hz tolerance only flags an off-nominal selected peak; it does not constrain the search or noise band.

**Global peak / same-frequency background (supplement).** The same global frequency `f*` and amplitude `A` define signal power `A**2/2`. Each independent control is divided into complete nonoverlapping 6 s windows; tails are omitted and reported. After the same timestamp interpolation and demeaning, evaluate `C=sum(w*y*exp(-2j*pi*f*arange(N)/fs))/sum(w)` with periodic Hann weights, without searching noise frequencies. Background power is `2*abs(C)**2`. Average these powers in linear units within each control; do not pool controls or average dB. The ratio is `(A**2/2)/mean(background_power)`, with `10*log10` conversion and no subtraction. Nominal Hann ENBW is `1.5*fs/N`, approximately 0.25 Hz, with no added ±1 Hz integration. All planned complete windows must be supported; partial results remain auditable. These windows are not assumed independent experimental repeats.

The Figure 2d response is the ratio of global interpolated FFT peak amplitudes from independent IM and reference windows at equal nominal settings. Its counts/g interpretation remains conditional on reference comparability. Global maximum selection can favor interference/harmonics and bias weak signals; finite SNR does not establish calibrated sensitivity, detection limit or sensor bandwidth.

This display does not change SNR-threshold eligibility or detectable intervals. Standalone fundamental figures contain the same values as the first panels of the corresponding comparisons.

The notebook's `PLOT_YMAX` controls exact comparison-figure upper limits in top-to-bottom amplitude/SNR/responsivity order. The optional redraw cell creates a new presentation run from saved tables without modifying the full tracking parent or its input selection.

The current selection uses one sweep per enabled material and independent accelerometer sweep 3; deferred IM_58/60/62/63/64 are not analyzed. A broad-band STFT ridge initializes a data-fitted logarithmic trajectory. Its 15-420 Hz search limits are analysis bounds, not demonstrated response. Raw examples from IM_4 use 50/100/150 Hz centers and 200 ms original-sample windows.

At 81 logarithmic grid points, final amplitudes use original timestamps and a complete centered 0.8 s Hann kernel, with normalized trapezoidal-time weights and weighted intercept/linear-trend removal. With fitted sweep rate `k`, `phi=2*pi*f_center*expm1(k*tau)/k` and `z=2*sum(w*y_detrended*exp(-1j*phi))`; peak is `abs(z)` and RMS is peak/sqrt(2). No noise-frequency optimization is performed. This offline demodulation smooths changes within the window; a dominant harmonic can still misidentify the trajectory.

**Estimator-equivalent SNR.** Nonoverlapping baseline windows begin at 5 s, with up to 30 selected windows and at least four accepted. They use the same frequency, sweep rate, duration, detrending and kernel as the signal. Noise equivalent peak is `sqrt(mean(abs(z_noise)**2))`; the amplitude ratio is converted with `20*log10`, without noise-power subtraction. Nominal two-sided baseband Hann ENBW is `1.5/T=1.875 Hz`. The actual detrended kernel bandwidth `fs*sum(abs(h)**2)/4` is saved and must match within 3%; irregular timestamps and unknown analog response prevent treating it as calibrated physical bandwidth.

**Broadband SNR (separate).** Divide tracking peak/sqrt(2) by `sqrt(mean(var(y_noise, ddof=0)))` over the same accepted original-sample baseline windows. Noise is demeaned only, with no filtering; it represents the recorded AC band, not the tracking ENBW. Convert the amplitude ratio with `20*log10`.

Both controls remain separate. Group amplitudes are arithmetic means and sample SD across accepted independent sweeps; group SNR is the mean of per-record dB values. One sweep has undefined between-record SD. Shared noise and overlapping signal windows do not add repeats. Detectable intervals merge only adjacent accepted grid points with sufficient repeats and mean amplifier-on SNR strictly above 10 dB; gaps are not bridged and cutoffs are not interpolated. This reporting threshold is not a false-alarm-calibrated detection limit or intrinsic/acoustic bandwidth. Responsivity divides independent mean IM peak counts by mean reference peak g at common accepted frequencies.

## Data11 whole-sweep FFT comparison

This separate estimator is retained in the optional supplement. It is not executed by the main notebook or the six-main-notebook `--all` command.

The preceding tracking run supplies separate model-inferred 30-to-300 Hz crossing times for each recording; these are not measured triggers. Incomplete windows remain unavailable. Interpolate to `N=floor(T*record_mean_fs)` samples at `start+arange(N)*T/N`, remove the mean, and apply the configured boxcar window. One rFFT covers the whole sweep, with no padding by default. Peak-equivalent bin magnitude is `2*abs(X)/sum(w)`, with DC/Nyquist exceptions. This is a sweep spectrum, not instantaneous fundamental amplitude. Native spacing and boxcar ENBW are `1/T` (about 0.0332 Hz for a roughly 30.1 s window).

Separate noise windows start at configured 5 s and are complete/nonoverlapping, matching each signal's T, N, window and nfft. At least one is required. Noise is `sqrt(mean(A_noise**2))`; SNR is the signal/noise amplitude ratio with `20*log10`, without subtraction. Group SNR averages per-record dB. The 0.1 Hz reporting grid interpolates bin power before taking its square root; it adds no resolution. Strict and gap-interpolated diagnostic populations remain separate. Ratios require common trajectory support, both sensors' SNR/repeat gates, duration agreement within 1% and ENBW within 3%. Single-window noise uncertainty, harmonics, leakage, interpolation and different accepted populations limit comparisons with tracking.
